Writing and reporting

My work as a reporter, though primarily focused on data-heavy stories, has led me to report on a wide variety of issues and characters. From handicapped residents ticketed for parking outside their own homes to part-time professors living under the poverty line, to family farmers struggling amid rising inequality in Nebraska's agricultural sector, I've always found ways to tell the human stories behind the numbers.

I don't just write data stories, though. I started my journalism career as a narrative features reporter, writing stories about Navy midshipmen cleaning Memorial Stadium, a high school soccer star coping with depression and Muslim students battling Islamaphobia during the 2016 election, to name just a few. Besides such slice-of-life pieces and my more recent data reporting, I've also dabbled in arts and even sports.

Coding and visuals

Though I never expected to do much visual work, and certainly none that involved any technological skills, much of my work in college involved both. In the 2016-17 school year, I taught myself how to code in order to build www.woundsofwhiteclay.com, the award-winning investigation into liquor sales in the tiny town of Whiteclay, Nebraska, and the harmful impact they had on the nearby Pine Ridge Reservation. Besides building and maintaining the class' website, I also did a lot of behind the scenes data reporting and created graphics and interactives for the site.

Besides working on "The Wounds of Whiteclay," I spent two semesters as the web editor for "The Daily Nebraskan," the student newspaper at UNL. As web editor, I built two special project sites while also maintaining the publication's main website. I also created multiple interactives including a county map for my story on farm inequality for the Omaha World-Herald.

In February I started a six-month bootcamp in data analytics and visualization at the University of Minnesota-Twin Cities. Through that program I am deepening my knowledge and abilities in a broad range of programming languages and software programs including Python, SQL, VBA, R, HTML/CSS, Javascript, D3, MongoDB and more. For samples of my work for the course and past coding projects, check out my github page.
