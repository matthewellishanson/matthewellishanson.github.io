# The Biggest Winner of All

Has Hell frozen over? Anybody notice any flying pigs out there? Or did the New York Knicks seriously just win the NBA Finals?

Well, after checking my pulse and the NBA app for the 10th time in the last 24 hours to make sure I hadn't imagined the whole thing, it appears the answer is still yes: the Knicks are NBA champions for the first time in more than 50 years. The comebacks, the culmination of Jalen Brunson’s legend, the redemption and rejuvenation of oft-maligned co-stars Karl-Anthony Towns and Mikal Bridges, the courtside crowd of celebs that could rival the Met Gala, and of course, those thousands of fans going apeshit through the streets of Manhattan – all of it was real.

What a moment this has become—not just for New York, or for Knicks fans, or for the players themselves. Now that the confetti has settled and the center of the basketball universe is celebrating its first championship in generations, it's worth recognizing what a gift these playoffs, and especially these Finals, have been to the sport itself.

After an improbable, borderline magical championship run that turned New York into a weeklong basketball festival—and, in a few cases, an actual fire hazard—the Knicks have given the NBA something priceless. Even with the World Cup beginning this week across Canada, Mexico and the United States, basketball managed to seize the center of the sports conversation. Regardless of what anyone thinks about New York or the Knicks themselves, it's hard to deny the scale of this moment. In that sense, basketball won these Finals every bit as much as the Knicks did.

# The Perfect Finals Stage

It’s not just the size and passion of the Knicks fanbase that made this such a compelling postseason outcome for the NBA. It was who they beat, and how both teams got there. This wasn't just the basketball equivalent of the Red Sox or Cubs finally breaking through. It also featured the emergence of the player most likely to become the face of the league for the next decade. Victor Wembanyama arrived on the Finals stage as basketball's most compelling young superstar and, for the first time in his professional career, experienced a genuine setback under the brightest lights the sport has to offer.

Any time a league gets to pit its most compelling young superstar against its most iconic franchise on the biggest stage, it's about as good a Finals matchup as the sport could hope for. And when that title matchup ends with the biggest and most fanatic city for the sport in question claiming a long-awaited title like this one did, well, it doesn’t get much better than that for any professional sports league. And that’s before you throw in Taylor Swift and Timothee Chalamet as the faces of the victorious fanbase (I’ll refrain from commenting on whether that’s earned or deserved… but shoutout to Spike Lee, John Turturro, Ben Stiller, Larry David and Tracy Morgan).

# A Bandwagon Worth Building

During these Finals, I heard more non-basketball fans talking about the NBA than I have in years. Some of that interest will disappear as quickly as it arrived, such is the nature of bandwagons. But not all of it will evaporate over the summer. Some people, even those who tuned in just for the spectacle, the celebrities, the street parties or the social media buzz, will stick around. A championship run with this much buzz and clout around it almost inevitably creates new fans, and few championships have had the reach or visibility of this one.

I could get into the actual on-court action and the implications for a wounded Wemby and the Spurs as well as those for the victorious Knicks. Heck, I could rattle off a few thousand words on Towns’ journey to this moment alone – what a bittersweet moment his role in this championship has become for all of us Minnesota Timberwolves fans who watched him star and struggle through his first decade in the league before finding his basketball destiny in the Big Apple – or go on and on in similar depth about Brunson’s brilliance or Wembanyama’s incredible early dominance through his first career postseason. But that’s kind of besides the point of this piece, really.

What I really just want to emphasize and celebrate here today is that major moment of elation and sensation this Finals brought to basketball. It’s not every day that even my 90-something grandparents can talk to me about who won the NBA championship, or that celebrities and us commonfolk rub shoulders and mingle in the streets of a major American city. This was a special, special moment for the whole basketball world, and the momentum from it will shape the near future of the NBA. For that alone, it’s a heck of a win for anyone who loves this sport (yes, even the Spurs fans, who must know in their hearts this is only the beginning of their window for multiple championships under Wembanyama’s unprecedented dominance).

# A New York Celebration

Beyond basketball, this also felt like a celebration of New York itself—the endlessly messy, loud, diverse, multicultural city that remains one of the defining places in American life. Watching millions of people from every imaginable background pour into the streets to celebrate the same team felt especially meaningful at a moment when so much of our politics revolves around division, exclusion and fear. Say what you want about mixing sports and politics, but I think that's pretty damn cool.

As one fan famously quipped earlier this week: “My mayor is Muslim, my bagel is Jewish, my Christian Dior, Knicks in four.”

That prophecy needed an extra game to come true, but the sentiment survived intact.

So yeah, go Knicks.

Congratulations to New York.

And congratulations to the sport itself, because basketball may have been the biggest winner of all.
